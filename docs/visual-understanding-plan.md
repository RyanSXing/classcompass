# Visual understanding and teaching plans

## What changes

1. Lead with a visual learning picture for each skill. Use three plain stages: **Needs support**, **Getting there**, and **Works independently**. Keep **Not enough evidence** separate.
2. Derive stages from shown methods, explanations, task demands and recorded help. A correct answer alone is not proof of understanding. Show the evidence and rule behind every stage; do not invent an overall score or growth percentage.
3. Show the class over dated assignments, a student-by-date skill map, and an individual stage chart. Selecting a point opens its evidence and next teaching step. Missing work creates a gap, not a decline.
4. Put the same skill picture on student pages. Keep answer counts available in work details.
5. Make teaching suggestions short cards with time, action and a check for success. Show lessons as a timed sequence, with Do / Ask / Check blocks. Keep full instructions, examples and printable plans available.
6. Use short, familiar wording in both generated and saved content. Keep the purple/aqua design and teacher approval of changes.

## Checks

- Unknown help, blank answers and unclear readings cannot establish independent performance.
- Each skill uses its own criteria; an arithmetic slip cannot erase demonstrated renaming.
- Earlier views exclude later work. Replaced submissions and corrections use the current effective reading.
- Charts work with keyboard and touch, with text labels and accessible evidence links.
- New suggestions do not silently change saved lessons, lesson time or assessment dates.
- Verify at phone and desktop sizes, run domain tests and the teacher browser workflows, then build and push.

Stages describe the available work, not a permanent label or a standardized measure of learning.
